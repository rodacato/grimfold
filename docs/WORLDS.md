# Worlds

Grimfold's engine is world-agnostic: a world is data (Places, Classes, monsters, loot names, story) plus an art set. Three worlds were considered on 2026-10-22. **The MVP is built on World A.** B and C stay here as candidates for later volumes.

All three share one look: **paper cut-out collage made from public-domain engravings, moved like puppets**. That is the line from Lotte Reiniger's silhouette films (*The Adventures of Prince Achmed*, 1926) to Terry Gilliam's Monty Python animations (cut-outs of old engravings) to South Park (construction-paper cut-outs). We use their *technique*, never their characters.

> **Public domain, briefly.** In the US, everything published in 1930 or earlier is public domain as of 2026-01-01. A faithful photo of a public-domain 2D artwork adds no new copyright (US: *Bridgeman v. Corel*; EU: Directive 2019/790, art. 14). What is **not** free: modern translations, modern illustrations, film-specific designs and trademarks. Verify each source before it ships.

## A · Inferno — *the MVP world*

**One line:** you descend the nine circles of Dante's Hell, floor by floor. You are not Dante.

**Why it fits.** The book already is the game's structure:

- **Descent = Pisos.** Each circle is deeper and harder, so the minimum Nivel per Place comes for free.
- **Each circle has its own punishment = its own mechanic.** "Two Places that are very different mechanically" was a requirement; Dante wrote them.
- **Nine circles = an expansion roadmap in order.** Each one is a content pack.
- **Named demons with personality.** The Malebranche (Malacoda, Barbariccia, Farfarello…) are mocking and treacherous, almost comic. That suits cut-out puppets while staying grim.
- **Medieval** (written c. 1308–1321), as asked.

### Places (one per circle) — mechanic and boss ideas

| # | Place | Sin | Mechanic idea | Boss idea |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Antinferno (Vestibule) | The uncommitted | Tutorial. Wasps chase you, so standing still costs Vida | Charon, at the crossing of the Acheron |
| 1 | **Limbo** | The unbaptized, virtuous pagans | **Fog:** you only see the Salas next to you. Few fights, many choices and riddles | Minos, the judge at the threshold; his tail is a curse |
| 2 | Lust | Lust | **Wind:** some Salas push you to another Sala | The storm itself, or a pair of bound souls |
| 3 | **Gluttony** | Gluttony | **Filthy rain and mud:** every move drains the Recurso | **Cerberus**: three heads, three attacks a round |
| 4 | Greed | Avarice / prodigality | **Weight:** loot is heavy and slots have a carry limit | Plutus ("Pape Satàn aleppe") |
| 5 | Wrath | Wrath, sullenness | **Swamp (Styx):** sink if you stop. Wrathful souls also fight each other | Phlegyas, the ferryman, or Filippo Argenti |
| 6 | Heresy | Heresy | **City of Dis, burning tombs:** opening a tomb gives loot or fire | The Furies and Medusa (petrify = lose a turn) |
| 7 | Violence | Violence | River of blood, a wood of bleeding trees, burning sand: three rings, three sub-rules | The Minotaur |
| 8 | Fraud — Malebolge | Fraud | **Ten ditches:** each Sala is a different trick. The Malebranche lie to you | Malacoda; Geryon as the way down |
| 9 | Treachery — Cocytus | Treachery | **Ice:** you cannot move freely; each step is earned | Lucifer, three faces chewing |

**MVP candidates:** two circles that contrast most, with a minimum-Nivel gap between them. Recommended: **Limbo** (Nivel 1, fog, choices, Minos) and **Gluttony** (Nivel ~4, attrition, Cerberus). Wrath (swamp) is the alternative to Gluttony. Decided in the brief.

### Classes — MVP candidates (mechanically opposite)

- **The Penitent** — a flagellant. Spends her own **Vida** as power: blood buys hits. High risk, high reward; dies fast if greedy.
- **The Friar** — an exorcist with relics and a candle. Spends **Fe** (a depleting clock-and-weapon resource, the Veladora lesson). Slow, safe, runs out.

Who they are and why they descend is the story's first question (rescuing someone, collecting a debt, fleeing a sentence…). Decided in the brief.

### Loot flavor

Relics, indulgences, rosaries, blessed and cursed weapons, pilgrim badges.
Rarity draft: Común (gray) → Bendecido (blue) → Consagrado (gold) → Reliquia (unique) → sets named after saints or sinners.

### Sources (public domain)

| What | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Text | Dante, *Divina Commedia* (Italian original) | Our text is written fresh; quotes only from the original |
| English translation | Longfellow (1867) | PD |
| Spanish translation | Bartolomé Mitre (1890s) | Likely PD; verify edition |
| Main art | **Gustave Doré**, *Inferno* engravings (1861) | Wikimedia Commons |
| Hell map | Botticelli, *Map of Hell* (c. 1485) | Layout reference |
| Demons and grotesques | Hieronymus Bosch (*Last Judgment*, *Garden of Earthly Delights*), Bruegel the Elder (*Dulle Griet*, *Triumph of Death*) | Cut-out material |
| Death | Holbein, *Dance of Death* woodcuts (1538) | Strong silhouettes, perfect for puppets |
| Architecture | Piranesi, *Carceri d'invenzione* (1750s) | Impossible prisons for Dis and Malebolge |

### Risks

- Dante is famous (EA's *Dante's Inferno*, 2010). Our protagonist and story must be our own.
- Religious imagery: keep it literary and folk-medieval, not a statement.

## B · Gothic Victorian, with steam

**One line:** the monsters of 19th-century novels are loose under the cities of Europe, and you hunt them with machines.

**Why it fits.** It has the most imaginative free monster roster there is, and descent comes built in: Verne's *Journey to the Center of the Earth* is literally a dungeon dive.

### Places (ideas)

| Place | Source | Mechanic idea |
|---|---|---|
| The Paris Catacombs | Real ossuary; Poe's tone | Walls of bones: dead ends, collapsing passages |
| The London Sewers | Doré's *London: A Pilgrimage* | Flooding: water rises each Sala |
| The Opera's underground lake | Leroux, *The Phantom of the Opera* (1910) | Traps and a hunter who stalks you |
| The automaton factory | Patent drawings, Verne | Machines repeat patterns you can learn |
| Dr. Moreau's island | Wells (1896) | Beasts that change form mid-fight |
| The castle of the Count | Stoker, *Dracula* (1897) | Night and day: the boss heals in the dark |
| The center of the Earth | Verne (1864) | The deepest descent; prehistoric beasts |

### Classes (ideas)

- **The Hunter** — a steam-powered crossbow, consumable bolts, positioning.
- **The Alchemist** — drinks her own elixir: two forms, like Jekyll and Hyde, each with its own rules.

### Monsters (all PD as written in the novels)

Frankenstein's creature (1818), Carmilla (1872), Mr. Hyde (1886), Dracula (1897), the Invisible Man (1897), Moreau's beasts (1896), Martian machines (Wells, 1898), the Phantom (1910), Poe's horrors.

### Sources and cautions

- Art: Victorian engravings, Doré's *London* (1872), 19th-century patent drawings, Gilliam-style catalog cut-outs.
- **Avoid film designs**: Universal's Frankenstein look (flat head, neck bolts) is the studio's. Use the novel's description.
- Risk: steampunk is crowded in indie games, and it is not medieval.

## C · The Void Cathedral

**One line:** a gothic cathedral-ship drifts dead in the void. You board it and descend deck by deck.

**Why it fits.** Grimdark as a word comes from this tone: gothic cathedrals in space, machine priests, technology treated as faith. Boarding a derelict hulk, deck by deck, is a classic dungeon-crawl shape. Loot reads as sanctified relics with affixes.

### Places (ideas)

| Place | Mechanic idea |
|---|---|
| The zero-gravity nave | You drift: movement overshoots |
| The flooded coolant deck | Cold drains the Recurso |
| The plague sector | Infection grows each Sala |
| The machine sanctuary | Machines answer prayers or punish them |
| The navigators' crypt | Dead pilots whisper a map, sometimes lying |

### Classes (ideas)

- **The Armored Penitent** — steam armor, heavy, slow, unstoppable.
- **The Machine Priest** — prays to the ship; repairs, hacks, summons servitors.

### Sources and cautions

- Visual PD material: Piranesi's *Carceri*, gothic cathedral engravings, Victorian machinery engravings, Doré.
- **Tone inspiration only: Warhammer 40,000 and Space Hulk are Games Workshop property**, and the company defends its marks hard. Never use: Imperium, Astartes, Space Marine, Tyranid, Genestealer, Adeptus, Emperor of Mankind, or any of its faction or unit names.
- Risk: "40k without the names" is common in indie games; it needs its own twist.
